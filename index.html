The idea

Kai grew up in California rivers, and this one is now the family beach: he takes his kids to the swimming spot by the Henry Cowell picnic area. Every visit ends at the same bend, where the river slips out of sight downstream. On August 29 we keep going: three miles of ankle-to-knee water with a few chest-deep pools, a swimming hole every 45 minutes or so, and no trail to follow because the river is the trail. We get out where the park ends; the next thing downstream is a private Masonic community, and they are not expecting us.

The wade

  1. 11:30Meet at Kai's place, San Jose. Coffee, sunscreen, water-shoe inspection. Sandwiches are already in the cooler.
  2. 12:00Uber Black SUV (or XXL) to Felton. Drop-off: Henry Cowell Redwoods State Park visitor center, 101 N Big Trees Park Rd. Budget 75 minutes with Saturday beach traffic on Hwy 17.
  3. 13:15Arrive and eat. Sandwich lunch at the picnic tables, plus bathrooms and water fountains, the last reliable ones until dinner. The empty cooler folds flat into a pack.
  4. 14:00Feet in the water at the river beach by the picnic area. Wade under the Roaring Camp railroad trestle and into the canyon.
  5. 15:15Garden of Eden. The famous one: deep blue-green pool in a rock cove. Long swim stop and snacks.
  6. 16:25Cable Car Beach. Sandy, sunny, quieter than Garden of Eden.
  7. 17:05Big Rock Hole. A big rock, a deep hole. Feet-first jumps, sand beach, last swim.
  8. 17:35Exit at Rincon. From Big Rock Hole, Rincon Fire Road climbs a steep half-mile (~250 ft) up to Highway 9. Twenty sweaty minutes and we're out.
  9. 18:00Pickup at the Rincon parking lot on Highway 9. Pre-booked van (details below). Change into dry shirts, feel superior.

Times are examples. Everything after 14:00 depends on how long we linger at each hole.

Logistics, including the one hard problem

The hard problem: cell service. Coverage is poor from the trestle to the Rincon lot and spotty at the lot itself, so we don't plan on hailing an Uber from the exit. Instead:
  • Primary: pre-booked local taxi or van, arranged days ahead from home for "Rincon parking lot, Highway 9, 18:00." Driver shows up whether or not we have bars.
  • Backup: Uber Reserve, scheduled for the same time and place. Once someone finds signal, we cancel whichever ride we don't need.
  • Floor: our own feet. If no car shows by 18:20, we walk down Highway 9 toward town. Signal tends to come back near the golf course, a mile or two down, and downtown is only 3 miles.
  • Group rules: phones ride in Kai's dry bag, and they'd be useless in the canyon anyway. Screenshot this page and the map. Tell partners we're off-grid 13:00-18:00.
  • Ride costs (estimates, surge varies): down in a Black SUV $150-200, or XXL $85-110; Rincon pickup van $40-60; home in a Black SUV $170-220, or XXL $95-125. Split six ways: about $40-50 each the XXL way, $60-80 each riding Black.
  • Park entry: the $10 day-use fee is per vehicle; arriving by Uber, we may owe nothing. Bring a little cash regardless.
  • Packing note: alcohol and glass are banned in the park, and rangers do ticket. Water on the river; drinks at the restaurant.
  • Weather and water, checked Aug 24: NWS calls for sunny and a high near 83 in Felton, and the USGS Big Trees gauge reads 18 cfs, ideal wading. Final check two days out.

What to bring

  • River footwear, the one item that matters. Best: closed-toe water shoes (Astral Loyak or Brewer) or a strapped river sandal (Keen Newport H2, Bedrock Cairn). In a pinch: old sneakers with real tread and thin wool socks. Not on this river: flip-flops, slides, smooth soles, or bare feet.
  • Swim trunks and a quick-dry shirt (long sun-exposed stretches).
  • Dry shirt, shorts, and socks for dinner. You'll want them.
  • Optional: compact towel, a found stick for the deep bits.
  • 1.5-2 liters of water each, plus salty snacks.
  • Sunscreen, hat, sunglasses with a strap.
  • Bags: two or three small backpacks across the group is plenty. Volunteers wanted.
  • Kai brings: lunch sandwiches for the crew, a dry bag for everyone's phones, wallets, and keys, plus first aid kit, spare ziplocks, and snacks. Zero glass.
